The 5-Step Process

Follow these steps in order. Don't skip ahead — each step builds on the last.

1

Build Your Emergency Fund

Before investing anything, save 3–6 months of living expenses in a high-interest savings account. This protects you from debt when unexpected costs hit (laptop breaks, car repairs, sudden job loss).

2

Open Betashares Direct

Create a Betashares Direct Individual account (you're an adult, not using the Kids account). Quote your Tax File Number (TFN) to avoid extra withholding tax on earnings.

3

Fund Your Account

Set up recurring bank transfers to your Betashares Direct Wallet using the BSB and account number provided. Osko often makes BSB transfers instant. PayID is not advertised on Betashares Direct — use BSB + account number for reliability.

4

Buy Core ETFs

Start with Australian and global index ETFs. Use Manual DIY mode (zero platform fee, zero brokerage) while balances are small. Focus on ASX-listed ETFs to avoid foreign exchange fees on small amounts.

5

Automate & Leave Alone

Set up Auto-Invest or recurring deposits, then leave it alone. Review once a year. Don't check daily — time in the market beats timing the market.

Why an Emergency Fund?

🛟 Your Financial Safety Net

An emergency fund is money set aside to cover unexpected expenses or loss of income. It's your first priority before investing.

What it protects you from:

  • Unexpected expenses: Laptop dies, car repair, medical bill
  • Income loss: Job ends, hours cut, client work dries up
  • Forced bad decisions: Selling investments at a loss or taking on high-interest debt

How much to save:

  • Minimum: $2,000–$3,000 to handle common emergencies
  • Target: 3–6 months of fixed living expenses (rent, food, transport, bills)
  • Where to keep it: High-interest savings account (e.g., UBank Save 4.60%* p.a., ING, Macquarie)

*Rates change frequently. Check current rates before opening an account.

⚠️ Don't invest before you have an emergency fund.

If an emergency hits and you have to sell investments to cover it, you might sell at a loss. The market doesn't care about your timing.

CHESS vs Custodial Holdings

📋 Quick summary:

Betashares Direct = Custodial (no personal HIN). This is normal and safe, but you should understand what it means.

Feature CHESS-sponsored (HIN) Custodial (Betashares Direct)
Your name on registry? ✅ Yes, shares in your name ❌ No, held by Betashares as custodian
Personal HIN? ✅ Yes (starts with X) ❌ No personal HIN
Broker portability ✅ Easy to transfer between brokers ⚠️ Sell & rebuy to move elsewhere
Safety if broker fails ✅ Shares are yours, held by CHESS ✅ Protected as client assets (segregated), but not on CHESS registry
Fractional shares ❌ Usually no ✅ Yes (Betashares Direct supports this)
Brokerage cost Varies ($0–$20/trade typical) $0 on Betashares Direct

So is custodial safe?

Yes, for most people:

  • Betashares holds your ETF units as custodian in a separate trust structure
  • If Betashares were to fail, your holdings are segregated client assets, not company assets
  • You can still see your holdings and receive dividends normally

When might you want CHESS later?

  • You want full control and portability across brokers
  • You're investing in individual shares (not just ETFs)
  • You have a large portfolio and want direct registry ownership

Glossary (Click to Expand)

ETF (Exchange Traded Fund) +

A fund that holds a basket of assets (shares, bonds, etc.) and trades on the stock exchange like a regular share. When you buy an ETF, you own a slice of everything inside it. Example: VGS holds ~1,400 global companies.

Common mix-up: People sometimes type "EFT" (electronic funds transfer). The investment product is ETF.

Fractional Shares/Units +

The ability to buy less than one full unit of an ETF or share. For example, if VGS costs $120/unit and you have $50, fractional investing lets you buy 0.4167 units. Betashares Direct supports this; many CHESS brokers don't.

MER (Management Expense Ratio) +

The annual fee charged by the fund manager, expressed as a percentage of your investment. Example: A200 has a 0.04% p.a. MER — on $1,000 invested, that's $0.40/year. Lower MER = more of your money stays invested.

Brokerage +

The fee you pay each time you buy or sell shares/ETFs. Betashares Direct charges $0 brokerage. Traditional brokers might charge $5–$20/trade. On small amounts, high brokerage eats into returns.

DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging) +

Investing a fixed amount regularly (e.g., $200/month) regardless of market price. You buy more units when prices are low, fewer when high. Smooths out volatility and removes emotion from timing.

DRP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan) +

Automatically reinvests dividends to buy more units instead of paying cash. Compounds growth but may trigger small taxable events. Check if your broker/platform supports it.

TFN (Tax File Number) +

Your unique Australian tax identifier. Quote it when opening investment accounts to avoid extra withholding tax (up to 47% on income if not quoted). Keep it private — never share publicly.

Franking Credit +

A tax credit for company tax already paid on dividends from Australian shares. If you're in a low tax bracket, you may get a refund from franking credits. Only applies to Australian shares (not international).

More info: ATO: Franking Credits for Individuals

Yield vs Total Return +

Yield: Income (dividends) as a % of your investment. Example: 4% yield on $1,000 = $40/year.

Total return: Yield + capital growth. An ETF might yield 3% but grow 7% in price = 10% total return. Focus on total return, not just yield.

Hedged vs Unhedged +

Unhedged (e.g., VGS): You're exposed to currency movements. If the AUD falls, your international holdings are worth more in AUD (and vice versa).

Hedged (e.g., VGAD): Currency risk is hedged back to AUD. You only get the share price movement, not currency swings. Hedging costs a small fee.

Most young investors use unhedged for long-term diversification.

Auto-Invest (Betashares) +

A Betashares Direct feature that automatically buys up to 5 Betashares ETFs on a schedule (weekly/fortnightly/monthly) using fixed dollar amounts. Zero fees, zero brokerage. Example: $100 into A200 + $100 into BGBL every month.

Custom Portfolio (Betashares) +

A Betashares Direct service that auto-rebalances your portfolio to maintain target percentages (e.g., 50% A200, 50% BGBL). Costs ~$3–4.50/month depending on balance. Only worth it for portfolios above ~$10k due to fee drag.

CHESS (Clearing House Electronic Subregister System) +

Australia's electronic share settlement system. When you have CHESS-sponsored holdings, your shares are registered in your name with a personal HIN (Holder Identification Number). Betashares Direct is custodial, so no personal HIN.

HIN (Holder Identification Number) +

A unique number (starts with X) that identifies you on the CHESS system when you own shares directly. Betashares Direct users don't get a personal HIN because holdings are custodial.

Custodial +

When a broker or platform holds investments on your behalf as custodian. You own the assets, but they're registered in the custodian's name (for your benefit). Betashares Direct is custodial. Your holdings are segregated and protected.

ASX (Australian Securities Exchange) +

Australia's primary stock exchange where shares and ETFs are listed and traded. "ASX-listed" means you can buy it on the Australian market in AUD without foreign exchange fees.

BSB vs PayID +

BSB: Bank-State-Branch number (6 digits) used to identify a bank account. Betashares provides a BSB + account number for your Wallet.

PayID: A simpler identifier (email/phone) for instant transfers. PayID is not advertised on Betashares Direct — use BSB + account number.

Osko: The instant payment system that often makes BSB transfers instant if both banks support it.
